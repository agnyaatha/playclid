# Learn Snooker (and Pool) on YouTube: An 8-Week Beginner Playlist

Build your course mainly from **Barry Stark Snooker Coach**, a Nottingham-based senior World Snooker coach whose YouTube bio says he has coached 2024 world champion Kyren Wilson from a young age. He has 90+ short, numbered tutorials covering almost every module you need.\[1\] Fill the gaps with **Stephen Hendry's Cue Tips** (snooker) and **Dr. Dave Alciatore** (pool physics, which also applies to snooker).

## TL;DR
- **Core spine:** watch Barry Stark's numbered series in the order below. Add Hendry for fixing faults and getting out of snookers, and Dr. Dave for cue-ball physics.
- **Plan:** about 50 videos over 8 weeks. Do about 3 table sessions a week, and spend more time at the table than watching.
- **Honest limits:** every link below was checked against a real YouTube page or the coach's own index. I could not read exact durations because YouTube blocked automated access, so the lengths shown are estimates.

## Module 0 — Rules, table, equipment
**Goal:** know the balls, values, fouls and the kit before you touch a cue.

1. **The Rules of Snooker – EXPLAINED!** — Ninh Ly [S]
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CjSWUTkupQo
   *Why:* a clear beginner walkthrough of potting, snookers, fouls, free balls and the 147.\[3\]
2. **The Basic Rules of Snooker** — Snooker Canada [S]
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_nfi9cB6okQ
   *Why:* made by a national governing body. Reds are worth 1, then yellow 2 up to black 7,\[4\] and fouls cost a minimum of 4.\[5\]
3. **The Rules of 8 Ball Pool – EXPLAINED!** — Ninh Ly [P]
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eDKt-kaOtGE
   *Why:* the same clear style, for pool players.\[6\]
4. **HOW TO PLAY 8 BALL … The "Official Rules" of Pool** — DrDaveBilliards [P]
   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c-4URzvawhs
   *Why:* he argues the official rules beat "bar rules".\[7\] (I only saw this one as a search result; the page didn't load.)
5. **BS #47 The Cue – Part 2 Important aspects** — Barry Stark [B]
   https://youtu.be/y1O6qdtAmus
   *Why:* explains what matters on a cue, especially the tip end.\[1\]

**Practice:** set up a snooker table from memory, with every colour on its spot. Then explain to a friend what happens after a foul.

## Module 3 — Aiming and potting
**Goal:** pot straight balls reliably, then gentle cuts.

20. **BS #29 Potting Line Pt 1 – Ghost ball theory** [B] — https://youtu.be/8zPOMH_effA
21. **BS #30 Potting Line Pt 2 – Aiming along the line** [B] — https://youtu.be/Fs2XZWyjVKk
22. **BS #20 Cueing Straight – Practice Exercise** [B] — https://youtu.be/P5zFwSQ-ZJs
23. **BS #41 Practice Straight Cueing** [B] — https://youtu.be/TxjTAmP-9B4

*Why:* Barry teaches ghost ball as a useful sighting aid. He warns that it isn't always accurate and depends on cueing straight, which is a useful correction to aiming "systems".\[1\]

**Practice:** set up 10 straight pots of medium length and count how many you make. Then try 10 half-ball cuts. Write both scores down every session.

## 8-week plan (about 3 table sessions a week, 60–90 min each)
- **Week 1:** Modules 0–1, plus the first half of Module 2 (grip, bridge, cueing parts 1–2). *Target:* a straight practice stroke over the spots.
- **Week 2:** Finish Module 2. *Target:* 6/10 medium straight pots.
- **Week 3:** Module 3. *Target:* 8/10 straight pots and 5/10 half-ball cuts.
- **Week 4:** Module 4, without side yet. *Target:* stun-stop the white 7/10 times.
- **Week 5:** Finish Module 4 (side), plus Module 7 (rests). *Target:* 5/10 pots with the rest.
- **Week 6:** Module 5. *Target:* a break of 10+, and clear the colours with no more than 2 misses.
- **Week 7:** Modules 6 and 8. *Target:* 3 of 5 snookers escaped, and a fixed weekly routine in place.
- **Week 8:** Modules 9 and 10. *Target:* review your filmed technique and play 3 full frames.

If you're behind, repeat a week. Fundamentals matter more than the schedule.

## Beginner equipment
- **Use the house cue at first.** Buy one only after a few weeks, once your stroke is consistent.
- **Snooker cue:** about 57–58 in, 17–18 oz, tip about 9.5 mm, two-piece (¾ joint) ash. Expect £50–£100 for a decent starter.\[20\]
- **Pool:** English pool uses tips of about 8.5–9.5 mm, so one snooker cue can do both. American pool cues have 12–13 mm tips.\[21\] Use a separate cue if you play US 9-ball.
- **Tip:** medium hardness, dome-shaped. Barry's #52–53 cover changing and caring for a tip.\[1\]
- **Chalk:** a cube of quality chalk. Chalk lightly before most shots.
- **Extras:** a hard case, and a glove if your bridge hand sticks. Don't buy gadgets until month 2.
- **Rules:** the official WPBSA rulebook only sets a minimum cue length of 3 ft (914 mm), so 57–58 in is a convention, not a rule. The rulebook covers snooker and English billiards (free PDF at wpbsa.com/rules).

## Caveats
- **Durations:** YouTube blocked automated reads of video length. The only figures given come from chapter markers or the creator's own description.
- **How links were checked:** most Barry Stark links come from the index on his own website, which links each episode number to its video.\[1\] YouTube could add, rename or remove videos later.
- **Age of videos:** many of Barry's core videos date from about 2016.\[2\] The technique is timeless, but they don't look new.
- **English billiards:** I found no strong beginner YouTube course. Use the WPBSA rulebook, and Barry's cannon drill (#42) as a bridge.
- **Etiquette:** no strong video exists for this, so rely on the basics in Module 1.
- **Nothing replaces a coach:** one or two lessons with a WPBSA-qualified coach in weeks 2–3 will catch faults that video can't.
